Reading the Retina

Development and Validation of a Deep Learning Algorithm for Detection of Diabetic Retinopathy in Retinal Fundus Photographs

Varun Gulshan, L. Peng, M. Coram et al. · 2016 · doi:10.1001/jama.2016.17216

Google researchers trained a neural network on about 128,000 photographs of the back of the eye, each graded by eye doctors, and showed on two separate test sets that it can tell which diabetic patients need to see a specialist almost as reliably as a panel of those specialists.

I · In 60 Seconds

Problemtoo few graders

Diabetes slowly damages the tiny blood vessels of the retina, the light-sensitive layer at the back of the eye. This diabetic retinopathy is a leading cause of blindness in working-age adults, and it is treatable if caught early. So diabetic patients are screened with a fundus photograph: a colour photo of the back of the eye, taken through the pupil. A trained reader grades it for small bleeds, leaks and swelling.

The question is whether the eye is referable: moderate or worse retinopathy, or swelling of the macula (the centre of the retina, which gives sharp vision). Either means the patient should see an ophthalmologist. There are far more photos than qualified readers, and readers often disagree with each other.

Idea128,175 graded photos

Train a convolutional neural network on graded photos. This is a stack of layers, each a structured linear map followed by a simple pointwise nonlinearity. They used Google's off-the-shelf Inception-v3 design, starting from weights already trained to recognise everyday objects. It never sees hand-drawn lesion outlines, only the photo and its grade.

The 128,175 training photos were each graded 3 to 7 times by a pool of 54 US ophthalmologists and senior ophthalmology residents. The majority grade became the label. The network outputs a score between 0 and 1, and a chosen threshold turns that score into "refer" or "don't refer".

ResultAUC 0.991

It was tested on two sets it never trained on: EyePACS-1 (9,963 photos of 4,997 patients from a US screening programme) and Messidor-2 (1,748 photos of 874 patients from France). Each photo was graded by at least 7 board-certified ophthalmologists.

Two terms: sensitivity is the fraction of referable eyes the network flags, and specificity is the fraction of healthy eyes it correctly lets go. The AUC (area under the curve) summarises the trade-off across every threshold; 1 is perfect and 0.5 is a coin flip. The AUC was 0.991 on EyePACS-1 and 0.990 on Messidor-2.

At a threshold tuned for few false alarms, sensitivity / specificity were 90.3% / 98.1% (EyePACS-1) and 87.0% / 98.5% (Messidor-2). At a threshold tuned to miss little, they were 97.5% / 93.4% and 96.1% / 93.9%.

II · The Linear Algebra

MachineryConvolution is circulant; Fourier diagonalises it

The paper is a clinical validation study, so it proves no theorems. The linear algebra is in the network it uses, and the core object is the convolution matrix.

The objects

\(x\in\mathbb R^{299\cdot299\cdot3}\)the photo

A fundus photo, resized so the eye is 299 pixels across, with three colour channels: a vector of 268,203 numbers.

\(x\mapsto \sigma(Cx+b)\)one layer

\(C\) is a learned matrix, \(b\) a learned offset vector, and \(\sigma(t)=\max(0,t)\) applied entrywise. The network composes dozens of these, then pools to a single score.

\(C\)sparse, weight-shared

Each output pixel depends only on a 3×3 patch of the input around it, through the same small set of weights (the kernel) at every location. Take an early Inception-v3 layer on a 147×147 grid, mapping 64 channels to 64. Written as a dense matrix, \(C\) would have \((147^2\cdot64)^2\approx1.9\times10^{12}\) entries. It has only \(3\cdot3\cdot64\cdot64=36{,}864\) free numbers.

One dimension, one channel: \(C\) is circulant

Strip it down to a signal \(x\in\mathbb R^n\) on a ring (indices mod \(n\)) and a kernel of three weights \(w_{-1},w_0,w_1\):

$$y_i = w_{-1}x_{i-1}+w_0x_i+w_1x_{i+1},\qquad C=w_{-1}S^{-1}+w_0I+w_1S,$$

where \(S\) is the cyclic shift, \((Sx)_i=x_{i+1}\), a permutation matrix and hence orthogonal. \(C\) is circulant: each row is the row above shifted one place right. It is symmetric exactly when \(w_{-1}=w_1\).

The spectral fact doing the work

Let \(\omega=e^{2\pi i/n}\) and \(f_k=(1,\omega^k,\omega^{2k},\dots,\omega^{(n-1)k})\), the discrete Fourier mode of frequency \(k\). Since \(Sf_k=\omega^kf_k\), every polynomial in \(S\) shares these eigenvectors:

$$Cf_k=\lambda_kf_k,\quad \lambda_k=w_0+w_1\omega^{k}+w_{-1}\omega^{-k},\qquad C=F\Lambda F^{*}.$$

Here \(F\) is the unitary matrix with columns \(f_k/\sqrt n\), and \(\Lambda=\operatorname{diag}(\lambda_0,\dots,\lambda_{n-1})\). The eigenvalues are the kernel's discrete Fourier transform. So every convolution acts as a frequency filter: it scales each Fourier mode by \(\lambda_k\) independently. Because \(F\) is unitary, \(C\) is normal, and its singular values are \(|\lambda_k|\).

The converse is why the structure matters: a matrix commutes with \(S\) iff it is a polynomial in \(S\) (the eigenvalues \(\omega^k\) of \(S\) are distinct), iff it is circulant. "Commutes with shifts" means a lesion is detected the same way wherever it sits in the photo. That is exactly what convolution, and nothing else linear, guarantees.

Tiny example: a blur on 4 pixels

Kernel \((w_{-1},w_0,w_1)=(\tfrac14,\tfrac12,\tfrac14)\), \(n=4\), so \(\omega=i\):

$$C=\tfrac14\begin{pmatrix}2&1&0&1\\1&2&1&0\\0&1&2&1\\1&0&1&2\end{pmatrix},\qquad \lambda_k=\tfrac12+\tfrac12\cos\tfrac{\pi k}{2}=1,\ \tfrac12,\ 0,\ \tfrac12 .$$

Check: \(C(1,1,1,1)^\top=(1,1,1,1)^\top\), since a blur keeps constants. \(C(1,-1,1,-1)^\top=0\): row 1 gives \((2-1+0-1)/4=0\). The blur annihilates the fastest alternation, so \(C\) is singular. The trace is \(2=1+\tfrac12+0+\tfrac12\). It is symmetric, so the eigenvalues are real.

Images, channels, and tensor products

On an \(n\times n\) image, shifts go two ways, and the 2-D convolution matrix is doubly block circulant. It is diagonalised by \(F\otimes F\), the Kronecker (tensor) product of two 1-D Fourier matrices. This is the same structure as a product unitary acting on two independent subsystems: the 2-D Fourier mode \(f_k\otimes f_\ell\) is a product state of a row frequency and a column frequency.

With \(c_{\text{in}}\) input and \(c_{\text{out}}\) output channels, \(C\) is a \(c_{\text{out}}\times c_{\text{in}}\) grid of such blocks. Conjugating by \(I\otimes F\otimes F\) turns it block diagonal, with one small \(c_{\text{out}}\times c_{\text{in}}\) matrix \(\hat W(k,\ell)\) per frequency. So the trillion-entry layer's singular values are the union of the singular values of \(n^2\) little matrices, and its operator norm is

$$\|C\|_2=\max_{k,\ell}\ \|\hat W(k,\ell)\|_2 .$$

Real layers pad the image border with zeros instead of wrapping around, so \(C\) is Toeplitz (constant along diagonals, no wrap). The Fourier picture is then exact in the interior and only approximate near the edges.

Separable kernels are rank one

A 2-D kernel is itself a small matrix \(K\). If \(K=uv^\top\) has rank one, convolving with \(K\) equals convolving with the column \(u\) and then the row \(v\). That costs \(2k\) weights instead of \(k^2\).

Inception-v3 builds this in: it replaces \(7\times7\) convolutions with a \(1\times7\) followed by a \(7\times1\). Without the nonlinearity between them, the pair would be exactly a rank-one \(7\times7\) kernel (14 weights instead of 49). With the nonlinearity, it is strictly richer, at the same cost.

IV · Cool Things

The AUC is a probability0.991

Pick one referable photo and one healthy photo at random. The AUC is exactly the probability that the network scores the referable one higher. This is the Mann–Whitney identity, and it needs no threshold. For two unit-variance Gaussians whose means are \(d\) apart, \(\text{AUC}=\Phi(d/\sqrt2)\), with \(\Phi\) the standard normal CDF. That is how Interactive 2 gets its \(d\approx3.35\).

98% specific, still 1 in 5 wrong flagsBayes

Of 1,000 screened eyes with about 8% referable, 90.3% sensitivity catches about 72 of 80. And 1.9% of 920 healthy eyes gives about 17 false alarms. So only \(72/89\approx 80\%\) of flags are real. Rare conditions punish false positives, which is why the paper reports a second, high-sensitivity threshold rather than one "accuracy".

Ten networks, averagedensemble

The final score is the plain average of 10 copies of the network trained on the same data. If their errors were independent with variance \(s^2\), the average's variance would be \(s^2/10\). They are correlated, so the gain is smaller, but averaging is a cheap, linear way to reduce variance.

The labels are a vote3–7 graders

The "truth" the network learns is the majority opinion of several ophthalmologists, because single graders disagree. A model trained on noisy majority labels can end up scoring near the consensus, more consistently than a typical individual on the panel.

V · If You Remember One Thing

The one thing to remember

A convolutional layer is a huge, sparse, weight-shared matrix that commutes with shifts, so the Fourier basis diagonalises it into a bank of frequency filters. Stacks of such filters, trained on 128,000 graded eye photos, picked out patients who need a specialist with AUC 0.99.
